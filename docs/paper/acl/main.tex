\title{\sys{}: Disaggregated Prefill and Decode Pruning\\ for Efficient Agentic Inference}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models perform agentic inference by expanding a committed prefix into multiple candidate residuals and retaining a single trajectory.
Existing search and early-stopping methods assign pruning scores only after tokens have been generated, so unpromising residuals still incur GPU prefill, KV transfer on a disaggregated connector, and decode.
To address this, we propose \sys, which identifies unpromising residuals during KV-cache encoding and defers continuation decisions to a short generated prefix.
We introduce two unshared scores with ordered thresholds $(\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}, \tau_{\mathrm{dec}})$: the prefill score reads residual text and withholds repeated loops before the kernel and before transfer, while the decode score reads a probed prefix and extends a residual only when that prefix exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.
The two scores do not share weights; collapsing them onto a single threshold discards trajectories that remain solvable.
Under disaggregation the connector inserts KV only for the admitted set.
Experiments on Qwen3-8B show that \sys reduces the prefill shared by three decode-time pruners while preserving any-finished accuracy, that a short probe restores full MATH-500 accuracy at lower end-to-end time, and that a three-turn Tree-of-Thoughts trace prefills $48$ of $192$ residuals.
\end{abstract}
