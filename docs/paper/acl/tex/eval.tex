\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:eval}

In this section we present experiments designed to validate the efficacy of \sys.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate on Qwen3-8B at temperature $0$ with prefix caching and tensor parallelism $1$, on four A100 40GB GPUs.
By default we use the ordered pair in Equation~\ref{eq:pair} and probe length $t_0{=}128$.
We evaluate GSM8K under three decode-time hosts and MATH-500 under the probe rule.
We further report peak KV, quality, and offered load on a serving stack whose live occupancy is the committed spine, using six model families.
Host parameters and metric definitions are given in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.
\sys is complementary to the hosts: it does not modify their scoring rules or introduce additional test-time model calls.

\paragraph{GSM8K hosts.}
Sixteen GSM8K problems, each a fan-out of $k\in\{4,8,16\}$ residuals with decode budget $D{=}512$.
Each fan-out is a controlled mix: half the residuals are viable strategy instructions, and half alternate a repeated loop with illegal text.
$\rho_0$ is always a viable strategy.
\emph{Any-finished} counts a problem if any admitted residual produces the reference number.
\emph{Winner} counts a problem if the residual selected as the winner does.
Both are reported over $16$.
ESC, speculative rejection, and DPTS are the decode hosts.
The host alone submits every residual; \sys applies $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ first and leaves the host cut in place.

\paragraph{MATH-500 probe.}
The first $32$ MATH-500 problems at level $4$ or higher, $k\in\{4,8,16\}$, $D{=}1024$.
Half the residuals are contest strategies and half are loops or illegal text.
The decode threshold is $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.
Full fan-out decodes every residual to $D$.
Discard drops every residual below that threshold before prefill.
Probe admits those residuals for $t_0$ tokens and extends only when the generated prefix exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.

Specifically, with the prefill gate disabled, the three hosts share one prefill of $5008$ tokens.

The gate removes loop prefill, which none of those cuts avoids.
This is a limitation of pruning only after tokens exist: the host can stop an unpromising residual, but it cannot recover $W$.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the two stages.
Decode pruning alone never changes prefill, and the time ranking is the ranking of the cut.
Prefill pruning alone, with no host cut, is already faster than ESC at $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and faster than all three hosts when the prefill threshold is $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.
Stacking a host on $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ still stops some admitted residuals.
Stacking a host on $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$ does not, because the admitted set is the viable half.
Any-finished holds throughout.
Winner accuracy is not identical to any-finished: withholding illegal residuals can change which residual the host ranks first.
The gate preserves a correct residual in the admitted set.
Appendix~\ref{app:width} reports $k{=}4$ and $k{=}8$, where the higher prefill threshold does reduce any-finished accuracy.

The two scores are ordered for a reason (Section~\ref{sec:scores}).
Illegal text lies above $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ on the residual and below $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$ on a prefix that is still illegal.
Using $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$ as the prefill threshold therefore withholds residuals that \eqref{eq:keep} would admit.
On MATH-500 that choice is the discard row of Table~\ref{tab:probe}.

\subsection{Disaggregated Search Traces}
\label{sec:trace}

The host experiments score one fan-out.
A search trace is a sequence of fan-outs on a committed spine.
We run three-turn Tree-of-Thoughts on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B at $\mathrm{tp}{=}2$, $n{=}16$, $k{=}4$, temperature $0$, one problem per generate.
Turns 1 and 2 decode a $32$-token step on the children admission kept.
The designated residual becomes the parent of the next turn.
Turn 3 answers on that spine, with $D{=}512$ on GSM8K and $D{=}256$ on Game24.
Across $16$ problems the trace offers $192$ residuals.
\sys queues the designated residual each turn and withholds the other three before the kernel, so $48$ residuals are prefilled.

Table~\ref{tab:mt} separates that expand from the winner decode.
On Qwen3-8B, GSM8K end-to-end time falls from $2.48$\,s to $1.91$\,s and Game24 from $2.15$\,s to $1.52$\,s.
GSM8K accuracy is unchanged on Qwen3-4B and one problem lower on Qwen3-8B.
Game24 accuracy rises on both models.
Peak KV on the live spine is $275$ tokens against $318$ for prefix caching on GSM8K, and $216$ against $265$ on Game24, because abort leaves the committed path.
Table~\ref{tab:best} is a different decision inside one uncommitted fan-out: retaining a single slot there drops siblings that still solve the problem.
The insert itself is \eqref{eq:xfer}.
On the loop-only accounting model, withholding one residual per session reduces the connector from $11520$ tokens to $8640$ (Appendix~\ref{app:xfer}).

The prefill gate does not lengthen the committed spine.
Appendix~\ref{app:spine} measures that spine against recomputation and automatic prefix caching.
Peak KV stays below the cache on six model families, because live occupancy is the spine.
On Qwen3-8B the pruned system keeps GSM8K quality with that cache and lowers end-to-end time.
Under a $1$\,s P99 time-to-first-token line, the shorter spine admits a higher offered load.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We presented a lightweight approach to efficient agentic inference.
By identifying unpromising residuals during KV-cache encoding, and by resuming an uncertain residual only when a short prefix exceeds a second threshold, \sys ensures that prefill and transfer are spent on viable strategies rather than on text that search will abort.
Experiments show a host-invariant reduction in shared prefill, lower end-to-end time, and preserved large-fan-out any-finished accuracy on GSM8K and MATH-500.
On a three-turn Tree-of-Thoughts trace the same gate prefills $48$ of $192$ residuals and shortens expand by $7.8$--$10.7\times$ relative to prefix caching.
Collapsing either decision onto the other threshold, or onto a single reserved slot inside an uncommitted fan-out, is what loses problems.
The split is complementary to decode-time hosts, prefix caches, and prefill--decode disaggregation: the connector inserts only the admitted miss.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limit}

The scores are surface tests: repetition, an illegal-math pattern, an answer mark, and character statistics.
They are not a learned reward model, and a residual that is wrong without being repetitive passes the prefill gate.
The evaluation mix is constructed so that half the fan-out is viable strategy text and half is a loop or illegal string.
It is a controlled test of the two thresholds, not a trace of a production agent.
Any-finished accuracy can stay flat while winner accuracy moves, and at $k{=}4$ even $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ drops one GSM8K problem.
The MATH-500 probe recovers full any-finished accuracy at $k{=}16$ and does not do so at $k{=}4$ or $k{=}8$.
The pruning study uses one model and temperature $0$, so the tables are single-run measurements.
The connector numbers in Appendix~\ref{app:xfer} are an accounting model, not a measured transfer time.
The three-turn trace admits the designated residual after the spine is committed, so it does not replace a learned value model, and Qwen3-8B GSM8K accuracy moves by one problem.
We do not explore training-based alternatives such as finetuning a pruner, since we target the setting without a heavy compute budget.
