\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Agentic inference expands a committed prefix into multiple candidate residuals and subsequently retains a single trajectory~\citep{tot,react,selfconsistency}.
This search-style generation has become standard for Tree-of-Thoughts, self-consistency, and tool-using loops.
The resulting accuracy gains, however, come at a substantial serving cost: every residual incurs GPU prefill, KV transfer on a disaggregated connector, and decode until a pruning signal becomes available.

\paragraph{Why prune before decode?}
Early-stopping self-consistency (ESC), speculative rejection, and dynamic parallel tree search (DPTS) terminate unpromising paths once generated tokens can be scored~\citep{esc,specrej,dpts}.
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:problem}(a), this policy still encodes every residual, so three hosts share one prefill.
Automatic prefix caching records a block only after it is full~\citep{vllm,sglang}, and a disaggregated connector forwards every page the prefiller has computed~\citep{distserve,splitwise}.
Section~\ref{sec:background} places each of these decisions on the fan-out.
A single threshold on both prefill and decode can discard residuals that remain solvable once a prefix is observed.
Admission therefore reads residual text, and continuation reads a generated prefix.

\paragraph{\sys}
We introduce \sys, which decouples the two decisions with ordered thresholds $(\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}, \tau_{\mathrm{dec}})$, $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}} < \tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.
The prefill score is computed from the residual and withholds a repeated loop before any kernel or KV transfer, so the disaggregated connector inserts only the admitted set.
The decode score is computed from a generated prefix of length $t_0$ and extends a residual to the budget $D$ only when that prefix exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$.
The designated residual $\rho_0$ is always admitted, so pruning cannot erase the trajectory retained when no sibling survives.
We show that the split is host-invariant in prefill work: any decode-time pruner that scores only generated tokens shares one $W$, equal to the prefill of the admitted set.

The inference pipeline comprises three stages---admission, prefill, and probe-and-resume---shown in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}.
\sys leaves the host's decode cut unchanged and introduces no additional model calls at test time.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We identify a placement gap: search, decode-time pruning, prefix caching, and prefill--decode disaggregation all decide after tokens or pages exist, so three hosts with different cuts share one prefill and one transfer.
\item We propose \sys, disaggregated prefill and decode pruning with unshared thresholds $(\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}, \tau_{\mathrm{dec}})$. The prefill score gates KV encoding and the connector; the decode score gates a short probe.
\item We provide measurements showing that the prefill gate reduces shared prefill in front of ESC, speculative rejection, and DPTS while preserving any-finished accuracy, that a short probe restores full MATH-500 accuracy at lower end-to-end time, and that a three-turn Tree-of-Thoughts trace prefills $48$ of $192$ residuals.
\end{itemize}
