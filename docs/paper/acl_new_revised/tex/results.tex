\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Setup.}
All GPU runs use vLLM on A100-SXM4 40GB GPUs with \texttt{bfloat16} weights, temperature $0$, and prefix caching enabled.
Prefill and decode are physically disaggregated: \Pin and \Din run as separate vLLM instances connected by a KV connector, and admitted residuals reach \Din only through that connector.
We measure computed prefill $W$, decode tokens $D^{+}$, peak KV, and end-to-end wall-clock.
The trunk is warmed before every fan-out (regime R3), so prefill differences reflect residual admission only.
The admission experiments use Qwen3-8B at $\mathrm{tp}{=}1$ on a \emph{branching mix}: for each problem, half of the $k$ residuals are distinct solution strategies, a quarter are repetition loops, and a quarter are syntactically malformed (``illegal'') text.
GSM8K uses $n{=}16$ problems and $D{=}512$; MATH-500 uses the first $32$ problems of level $\ge4$ and $D{=}1024$.
Decoding ignores EOS with a fixed budget, so $D^{+}$ is determined by the policy.
Our primary metric is \emph{committed-answer accuracy}: the answer of the residual the harness commits (index $0$ for MATH-500, the host's selection for GSM8K) matches the reference.
We also report \emph{any-finished} coverage, i.e., whether some full-budget branch is correct, which upper-bounds what a better selector could commit.
With $n{=}16$, two runs of the identical ESC configuration at $k{=}16$ commit $11/16$ and $8/16$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:ablate-prefill}), so differences of up to three problems are within run-to-run variation.

\paragraph{Admission composes with decode-time pruners.}
\Cref{tab:main} (GSM8K block) places one admission policy in front of three decode-time hosts.
Without admission, ESC, Speculative Rejection, and DPTS prefill the same $5008$ tokens; they differ only in $D^{+}$ ($131072$, $98304$, and $78336$ tokens) because each reads generated text.
Reject-only admission at $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}{=}0.15$ rejects the four loops before any kernel and reduces $W$ to $3848$ tokens ($-23\%$) for every host, as \Cref{prop:acct} predicts.
Committed accuracy is unchanged for ESC and DPTS and two problems lower for Speculative Rejection, within the run-to-run variation stated above; coverage remains $16/16$.
The time reduction is largest for ESC ($-17\%$), which would otherwise decode the loops to the full budget, and small for hosts that already stop them early ($-5\%$, $-3\%$).
Blanket rejection at $0.45$ also removes the illegal residuals and is faster ($-36\%$ for ESC) at a cost of one to two committed problems at $k{=}16$.
\Cref{tab:prefill-bar} shows that the trade-off depends on width: at $k{=}4$ and $k{=}8$ blanket rejection loses two problems of coverage, while reject-only stays within one.

\paragraph{The probe recovers what blanket rejection loses.}
On MATH-500 (\Cref{tab:main}, lower block), blanket rejection halves the time of the full fan-out but lowers coverage from $20/32$ to $13/32$: some residuals scored as malformed nevertheless lead to correct solutions once decoded.
Reject-only admission recovers part of the loss ($16/32$).
Probing the uncertainty band with $t_0{=}128$ restores $20/32$ at $72.2$\,s, $0.69\times$ the full fan-out, and reduces $D^{+}$ from $524288$ to $393216$ tokens.
All $128$ probed residuals were extended, and no loop residual would have passed the extension threshold (Appendix~\ref{sec:ablate-math}), so on this mix the probe acts as a correction of the prefill-side score rather than as an additional filter.
Its cost is visible in $W$: at $k{=}16$, evicted probe pages are recomputed on extension and $W$ rises to $28736$ tokens, which \eqref{eq:acct} accounts for as $\Delta_{\mathrm{ext}}$; at $k\le8$ the overhead is below $300$ tokens.
The best probe length depends on width (\Cref{tab:grow}): $t_0{=}64$ is preferable at $k{=}8$, and at $k{=}4$ no probe matches the full fan-out.

\paragraph{Connector volume.}
\Cref{tab:connector} evaluates \Cref{prop:acct} for a fan-out with $k{=}4$, $L{=}256$, $\ell_i{=}32$, and one rejected loop.
In a cold regime the dominant term is trunk sharing, whose size depends on whether the baseline engine deduplicates concurrent siblings and whether its connector is residency-aware.
In the warm regime that characterizes multi-step agents, the trunk is already on $\Din$ and the only remaining saving is admission, here $25\%$ of the residual volume.
We therefore attribute connector savings to admission only in the warm column and treat the cold column as an upper bound that a deduplicating, residency-aware baseline largely closes.

\paragraph{Spine retention lowers peak KV.}
On the full GSM8K test set ($n{=}1319$, Qwen3-14B, $\mathrm{tp}{=}2$, $k{=}4$), the context tree commits the same answers as APC within one point ($0.845$ vs.\ $0.844$) at $0.72\times$ its median peak KV ($1030$ vs.\ $1430$ tokens; Appendix~\ref{sec:eval-gpu}).
This result is due to state management alone (\cowonly, i.e., \sys without admission), since admission does not change the committed spine.
The ratio follows $(L+\ell_\star)/(L+\sum_i\ell_i)$ and is therefore governed by $k$ and the trunk-to-residual ratio rather than by the model: it is $0.72$--$0.75\times$ on GSM8K and $0.57$--$0.61\times$ on Game of 24 across six families (\Cref{fig:eval-multi} in Appendix~\ref{sec:eval-multi}), and it falls from about $0.90$ at $k{=}2$ to $0.53$--$0.55$ at $k{=}8$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:eval-sens}).
The copy-on-write fan-out incurs its own overhead: its median latency is $140$\,ms, compared with $87$\,ms for an APC hash hit.

\paragraph{Multi-step agent sessions.}
We run a three-step Tree-of-Thoughts session ($k{=}4$ per step, $n{=}16$, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, $\mathrm{tp}{=}2$) in which each step's spine is the next step's trunk (\Cref{tab:mt}).
Admission runs before every fan-out and prefills $48$ of the $192$ residuals.
Peak KV is $0.86\times$ APC on GSM8K and $0.82\times$ on Game of 24 for both \cowonly and \sys.
Committed accuracy matches APC on Qwen3-4B GSM8K, is one problem lower on Qwen3-8B GSM8K ($15$ vs.\ $16$ of $16$), and is one to three problems higher on Game of 24.
The \sys runs additionally stop committed decode at a finished answer marker, so their end-to-end reductions ($0.70$--$0.77\times$ APC on three of four settings) combine admission with decode truncation and should not be attributed to admission alone.
