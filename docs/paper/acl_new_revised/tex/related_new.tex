\paragraph{Inference-time search and path pruning.}
Chain-of-thought elicits intermediate steps~\cite{cot}; Tree-of-Thoughts, self-consistency, and Monte Carlo tree search expand many partial solutions and then select~\cite{tot,selfconsistency,mctsllm}.
Accuracy and token cost both grow with the number of traces.
Decode-time path pruners cut that cost after generation has started: ESC stops once a window agrees~\cite{esc}, Speculative Rejection halts low-reward prefixes at a horizon~\cite{specrej}, DPTS early-stops children after a mini-step~\cite{dpts}, and STOP organizes such signals by source and learnability and trains an internal futility token~\cite{stoploss}.
EquivPruner merges semantically equivalent steps so that textually distinct but mathematically identical actions are not explored twice~\cite{equivpruner}.
All of these decide from generated or written text and are agnostic to where the KV cache was computed.
\sys is complementary: it adds a pre-kernel decision and a decode-side probe, while these decode-time pruners operate unchanged on the admitted set; a learned scorer such as STOP's could serve as $\mathrm{Score}_{\mathrm{dec}}$.

\paragraph{Prefix sharing and PD disaggregation.}
Orca schedules at iteration granularity~\cite{orca}; vLLM pages the KV cache and hashes full blocks for APC~\cite{vllm}; SGLang shares prefixes through a radix tree~\cite{sglang}; Sarathi chunks prefills to co-schedule them with decode~\cite{sarathi,sarathisplit}.
DistServe and Splitwise place prefill and decode on separate instances and move KV across a connector~\cite{distserve,splitwise}.
These mechanisms determine whether a trunk is shared and how pages move, but they serve every request they receive.
Whether $k$ siblings share the trunk depends on the cache regime (\S\ref{sec:regimes}); even when the trunk is fully reused, each residual's uncached suffix is still computed and transferred.
\sys uses residency on both instances as an admission input and treats the connector as the channel whose volume admission reduces.

\paragraph{Agentic serving, retention, and copy-on-write.}
Autellix and Hermes schedule at program granularity~\cite{autellix,hermes}; ThunderAgent and SAGA optimize workflows~\cite{thunderagent,saga}; Parrot, Ayo, and Pythia exploit application structure~\cite{parrot,ayo,pythia}; JITServe allocates bandwidth under heterogeneous SLOs~\cite{jitserve}.
InferCept, Continuum, and MORI retain KV across tool calls~\cite{infercept,continuum,mori}; ConServe disaggregates prefill and decode scheduling at conversation granularity from observed agent behavior~\cite{conserve}; Sutradhara overlaps tool execution with the prefill of a single tool-independent prefix~\cite{sutradhara}.
ForkKV is closest in mechanism: it shares a base KV cache copy-on-write across LoRA agents~\cite{forkkv}.
CachePrune edits attention state inside one request~\cite{cacheprune}; speculative decoding drafts \emph{output} tokens rather than a future prompt prefix~\cite{speculative,medusa,eagledraft}.
These systems place, schedule, or retain contexts that will be served; ConServe in particular disaggregates scheduling but not pruning.
\sys targets the complementary decision of which sibling continuations should be served at all, and at which PD stage that decision is made.
Its copy-on-write context tree is the state-management mechanism that supports those decisions for siblings of one agent step.
