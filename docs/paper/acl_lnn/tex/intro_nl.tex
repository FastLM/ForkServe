\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §1 Background — multi-branch agentic execution
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Agentic LLM applications interleave generation with branching.
A coding agent proposes several candidate edits or tool calls for the same repository context, executes one, and discards the rest~\cite{react,sweagent,openhands}; an inference-time search controller expands a Tree-of-Thoughts frontier or a self-consistency pool from one partial solution~\cite{tot,selfconsistency,cot}.
Specifically, at each step the serving system starts from a committed context, the \emph{trunk} $x_u$, and expands it into $k$ candidate continuations.
We call the candidate-specific suffix $\rho_i$ a \emph{residual}, so candidate $i$ is the sequence $x_u\Vert\rho_i$.
We focus on branch-select workloads in which one continuation is eventually committed as the \emph{spine} and becomes the trunk of the next step, while the remaining branches are discarded~\cite{gsm8k,math,esc}.
This pattern requires the system to serve a fan-out of candidates even though only one lineage continues.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §2 Why important — compute / communication cost under PD disaggregation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Branching improves search coverage, but it also multiplies serving cost.
A residual that must be expanded further processes any still-uncached input (\emph{prefill}) and then generates tokens (\emph{decode}).
Production stacks increasingly place these phases on separate instances under prefill--decode (PD) disaggregation, so the KV pages produced by prefill must also cross a connector before decode can begin~\cite{distserve,splitwise}.
Most residuals are eventually discarded, yet before they are pruned they may already have consumed prefill compute, KV transfer, and some decode.
As the agent repeats this fan-out over many steps, the cost of non-surviving candidates accumulates.
Stopping an unnecessary residual earlier is therefore a first-class opportunity for efficient PD-disaggregated agentic serving.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §3 Existing gap — path pruning vs.\ KV optimization
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prior work improves efficiency mainly along two axes: path pruning and KV-cache optimization.
Decode-time path pruners cut low-value branches after partial generation: ESC stops sampling once a window agrees~\cite{esc}, Speculative Rejection halts low-reward prefixes at a horizon~\cite{specrej}, DPTS early-stops children after a mini-step~\cite{dpts}, and STOP predicts futility from a short prefix~\cite{stoploss}.
These methods depend on generated text, so by the time they prune, residual prefill and the connector transfer have often already been paid and cannot be recovered.
KV systems attack redundant work on the shared trunk: automatic prefix caching (APC) in vLLM and RadixAttention in SGLang reuse resident trunk pages~\cite{vllm,sglang}, and agent schedulers with KV retention manage context across tool calls~\cite{autellix,continuum,mori,conserve,forkkv}.
Even when the trunk is fully shared, however, each residual's uncached suffix must still be computed and transferred---work that is wasted if that candidate is later discarded.
APC neither rejects a residual nor controls which pages cross the connector.
Combined with PD disaggregation, pruning therefore remains \emph{stage-local}: each stage can discard only work that has already reached it.
The gap is that existing methods reduce duplicate compute and later decode, yet leave the prefill and KV-transfer cost of low-value candidates largely untouched.
We ask how to cut that early-stage cost in PD-disaggregated agent serving without sacrificing committed-answer accuracy.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §4 Challenges — the two stages of disaggregated pruning
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Achieving this goal, however, presents two key challenges.

\paragraph{Challenge 1: Prefill-side admission.}
Before a residual runs, the signals that existing pruners rely on---generated tokens---are unavailable, and KV residency is split across instances: a page resident on the prefill side may still require transfer or recompute on the decode side.
The controller must decide, cheaply and from pre-execution information alone, whether each residual should be skipped, prefilled, transferred, or decoded.

\paragraph{Challenge 2: Decode-side probing.}
A candidate judged low-value before prefill may still reach a correct answer after further generation.
Aggressive early rejection saves resources but can hurt committed-answer accuracy; retaining every residual preserves accuracy but erodes the gain.
When the pre-execution signal is inconclusive, a short subsequent generation should refine the decision before the residual consumes a full decode budget.

These decisions also allocate KV pages with different lifetimes.
Siblings share the trunk but own distinct residual pages, so an aborted branch must free its private pages, and the committed spine must stay resident as the trunk of the next agent step.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §5 Disaggregated pruning + contributions
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Disaggregated pruning.}
Our key insight is to disaggregate pruning across the prefill and decode stages.
\sys performs early admission before prefill when sufficient information is available, defers uncertain decisions to decode-side probing, and uses lightweight KV state management to support branch reclamation and spine retention (\Cref{fig:system}).
On the prefill side, a controller assigns each residual one of $\{\Hit,\Admit,\Probe,\Reject\}$ before any kernel runs, using dual-side page residency and a prefill-side heuristic score (\S\ref{sec:app}).
Residuals whose scores fall in an uncertainty band are probed: they are prefilled, decoded for $t_0$ tokens, and extended only if the generated prefix clears a decode-side threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$ calibrated separately from the prefill-side threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ (\S\ref{sec:split}).
Existing decode-time pruners such as ESC, Speculative Rejection, and DPTS operate unchanged on the admitted branches.
Lightweight KV state management supports these decisions: a copy-on-write context tree shares the trunk across siblings, transfers only decoder-missing pages, frees residual pages on abort, and retains the committed spine (\S\ref{sec:kvstate}).

\paragraph{Results.}
On Qwen3-8B GSM8K with $k{=}16$, prefill-side admission in front of ESC, Speculative Rejection, or DPTS reduces computed prefill from $5008$ to $3848$ tokens ($-23\%$) for every decode-time pruner; committed-answer accuracy is unchanged, and ESC wall-clock falls from $16.49$\,s to $14.45$\,s (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
On MATH-500, aggressive prefill-side rejection lowers any-finished coverage from $20/32$ to $13/32$, whereas at $k{=}16$ a $128$-token probe increases coverage to $20/32$ at $72.2$\,s instead of $104.1$\,s.
The supporting state management, which retains only the committed spine, reduces median peak KV usage on the full GSM8K test set with Qwen3-14B to $0.72\times$ that of APC at matched committed-answer accuracy, and the ratio stays within $0.72$--$0.75\times$ across six model families.
All measurements run on physically disaggregated vLLM prefill and decode instances connected by a KV connector.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item We formulate pruning in disaggregated agentic serving as a per-residual, cross-stage decision problem, and make explicit the cache regimes and residency conditions under which APC does or does not share the trunk (\S\ref{sec:problem}).
\item We propose \emph{disaggregated pruning} and instantiate it in \sys{} with prefill-side admission and decode-side probing; lightweight KV state management supports branch reclamation and spine retention (\S\ref{sec:design}).
\item We demonstrate that prefill-side admission composes with three existing decode-time pruning methods and reduces computed prefill, and that decode-side probing mitigates the coverage loss of aggressive prefill-side rejection, while preserving committed-answer accuracy (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
