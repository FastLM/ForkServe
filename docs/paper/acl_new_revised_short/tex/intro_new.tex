\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §1 Background — multi-branch agentic execution
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Agentic LLMs are widely used for complex reasoning, software development, and related tasks, and they often complete a problem by exploring multiple candidate plans rather than committing to one~\cite{react,sweagent,openhands,tot,selfconsistency,cot}.
Specifically, at each step the serving system starts from the current committed context and produces $k$ candidate branches; each branch can be independently expanded or evaluated as a subsequent reasoning request.
These requests therefore share a common context---the \emph{trunk}---but carry distinct candidate content---the \emph{residuals}.
After exploration and selection, typically only one path---the \emph{spine}---is retained and becomes the trunk of the next step, while the remaining branches are discarded~\cite{gsm8k,math,esc}.
This multi-branch exploration pattern requires the system to serve multiple candidate requests, even though only one lineage continues.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §2 Why important — compute / communication cost under PD disaggregation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Although branching expands the space of possible solutions, it also increases the resource cost of inference serving.
For a candidate that must be expanded further, the system processes any still-uncached input context (\emph{prefill}) and then generates subsequent tokens (\emph{decode}).
Under prefill--decode (PD) disaggregation these two phases run on separate instances, so the KV cache produced by prefill may also need to be transferred to the decode instance~\cite{distserve,splitwise}.
Most candidates are eventually discarded, yet before they are pruned they may already have consumed prefill compute, KV transfer, and decode resources.
As the agent repeats multi-branch exploration over many steps, the extra cost of non-surviving candidates accumulates.
Early identification and termination of unnecessary candidate execution is therefore an important opportunity for efficient PD-disaggregated agentic LLM serving.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §3 Existing gap — path pruning vs.\ KV optimization
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Prior work improves LLM inference efficiency mainly along two axes: path pruning and KV-cache optimization.
Path pruning methods such as Speculative Rejection stop low-reward branches after generating a short prefix~\cite{specrej}, and DPTS early-stops low-value paths after a partial reasoning step~\cite{dpts}, thereby reducing subsequent decode cost; related decode-time hosts include ESC and STOP~\cite{esc,stoploss}.
These methods, however, depend on already-generated content, so by the time they prune, residual prefill and KV transfer may already have been paid and cannot be recovered.
KV-cache optimization systems such as vLLM and SGLang reduce redundant compute and data movement through prefix reuse, distributed KV management, and cache-aware scheduling~\cite{vllm,sglang}, and agent schedulers further retain or share context across tool calls~\cite{autellix,continuum,mori,conserve,forkkv}.
Even when the shared prefix is reused, however, each uncached residual must still be computed and transferred independently---work that is wasted when that candidate is later discarded.
Thus, existing methods reduce duplicate compute and later generation, yet still leave the prefill and KV-transfer cost of low-value candidates largely untouched before pruning takes effect.
This raises a research question: how can PD-disaggregated agent serving cut the execution cost of low-value candidates earlier, while preserving reasoning quality?

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §4 Challenges
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Achieving this goal, however, presents three key challenges.

\paragraph{Challenge 1: Low-overhead pre-execution decision.}
Before a candidate truly executes, it is difficult for the system to decide at low overhead which work can be skipped.
Specifically, existing pruners rely on subsequent generation outcomes that are unavailable before execution; moreover, prefill and decode instances maintain KV caches separately, so a candidate whose pages are resident on one side may still require compute or transfer on the other.
The controller must therefore use pre-execution signals---together with dual-side KV residency---to decide quickly whether each candidate should run and how.

\paragraph{Challenge 2: Balancing pruning efficiency and reliability.}
Early pruning trades efficiency against reasoning quality, because a candidate's current score need not reflect its eventual value.
Specifically, some low-scoring candidates may still reach a correct answer after further generation: aggressive rejection saves more resources but can hurt quality, while overly conservative retention erodes the pruning gain.
Candidates of different certainty therefore need different degrees of exploration, using a short subsequent generation to refine the decision and balance compute cost against reasoning quality.

\paragraph{Challenge 3: Pruning-aware KV state management.}
Dynamic pruning complicates KV management, because siblings share part of the KV yet have different lifetimes.
Specifically, branches share the trunk's KV but own distinct residual pages: aborting a branch must free its private pages without dropping pages still referenced by live siblings, while the committed spine must remain resident for reuse in the next agent step.
The system must therefore coordinate sharing, timely reclamation, and cross-step retention of branch KV, avoiding both redundant storage and incorrect release.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% §5 System + contributions
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
To handle these challenges, we design \sys, which places a \emph{pre-kernel admission} policy, a \emph{decode-side probe}, and a \emph{copy-on-write context tree} across the PD path (\Cref{fig:system}).
Specifically, (i)~pre-kernel admission scores each residual with a model-free text score and dual-side KV residency, and assigns one of $\{\Hit,\Admit,\Probe,\Reject\}$ before any kernel runs (\S\ref{sec:app}); (ii)~the decode-side probe prefills uncertain residuals, decodes them for $t_0$ tokens, and extends them only if the generated prefix clears a threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{dec}}$ calibrated separately from the prefill-side threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{pre}}$ (\S\ref{sec:split}), while decode-time hosts such as ESC, Speculative Rejection, and DPTS operate unchanged on the admitted set; and (iii)~the copy-on-write context tree aliases the trunk across siblings, transfers only decoder-missing pages, frees residual pages on abort, and retains the committed spine for the next agent step (\S\ref{sec:state}).
We call this cross-stage placement \emph{PD pruning disaggregation}: the pruning decision, its execution, and the management of pruned state sit where each is cheapest and most informative.

\paragraph{Results.}
On Qwen3-8B GSM8K with $k{=}16$, admission in front of ESC, Speculative Rejection, or DPTS reduces computed prefill from $5008$ to $3848$ tokens ($-23\%$) for every host; committed-answer accuracy is unchanged, and ESC wall-clock falls from $16.49$\,s to $14.45$\,s (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
On MATH-500, rejecting every low-scoring residual before prefill lowers any-finished coverage from $20/32$ to $13/32$, whereas a $128$-token probe increases coverage to $20/32$ at $72.2$\,s instead of $104.1$\,s.
On the full GSM8K test set with Qwen3-14B, the retained spine requires $0.72\times$ the median peak KV of APC at matched accuracy, and the ratio stays within $0.72$--$0.75\times$ across six model families.
All measurements run on physically disaggregated vLLM prefill and decode instances connected by a KV connector.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item We formulate pruning in disaggregated agentic serving as a per-residual, cross-stage decision problem, and make explicit the cache regimes and residency conditions under which prefix caching does or does not share the trunk (\S\ref{sec:problem}).
\item We design \sys with pre-kernel admission, a decode-side probe, and a copy-on-write context tree, and we give a work and transfer accounting that separates trunk sharing, admission, and probe overhead (\S\ref{sec:design}).
\item We show on Qwen3 models that admission composes with three decode-time pruners, that a decode-side probe mitigates the coverage loss of aggressive prefill-side rejection, and that spine retention lowers peak KV, while reporting committed-answer accuracy throughout (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
